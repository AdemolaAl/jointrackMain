# Vertical: Nutra and Health (Supplements, Beauty, Wellness)
> Use when: someone runs or plans traffic for supplements, beauty, wellness or health-adjacent products: COD vs straight sale vs trial, policy pitfalls, consultation funnels in Telegram/chat apps, and metrics like approve rate and buyout.

Nutra (nutraceuticals) covers supplements, beauty, hair, skin, joint, weight management and similar products. It is a mature affiliate vertical with strict ad policies because of health claims. The products and claims must be legal in the target country. Joe helps with compliant marketing only; it does not help with medical claims, fake doctors or fake reviews.

---

## 1. Offer types

| Model | How it works | You are paid on | Key risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| COD (cash on delivery) | User leaves name + phone; call centre confirms; courier delivers; user pays on delivery | Approved lead (confirmed order), sometimes on buyout | Low approve or buyout rates; payout cut for bad leads |
| Straight sale (SS) | User pays online by card or local method | Sale | Lower conversion in cash-heavy markets; chargebacks |
| Trial / rebill | Small first payment, then recurring charges | Initial sale | High chargeback and complaint risk; many "trial" offers rely on hidden recurring billing, which breaks consumer law and platform rules. Joe only supports trials with clear, upfront terms and easy cancellation. |
| Lead gen (consultation) | User books a consultation | Qualified lead | Lead quality disputes |

### COD in detail
- **Lead**: form submission (name, phone).
- **Approve rate**: share of leads the call centre confirms as real orders. Driven by lead quality (real intent, correct phone number), call centre speed and the offer price.
- **Buyout rate**: share of approved orders that are actually paid for on delivery. Driven by honest expectations, delivery speed, and price surprises.
- Programs usually pay per approved lead and may adjust payouts if your buyout rate is low. Check the deal.
- Joinvoo statuses: `lead` for the form, `qualified` for the approve (if reported), `sale` for a paid order, `rejected` for cancelled/returned.

## 2. Policy pitfalls (Meta, TikTok, Snapchat; check current policies)

### Common rules across platforms
- **No before-and-after images** for weight loss, skin, hair and similar personal health results. Meta prohibits them in this context; TikTok and Snapchat are similar.
- **No personal attributes**: do not assert or imply someone's health condition ("Are you diabetic?", "Struggling with your belly fat?"). Speak about the product, not the viewer's body.
- **No negative self-perception**: no body shaming, no zooming on body parts to make people feel bad.
- **No unrealistic or medical claims**: no "cures", "treats", "reverses", "lose 10 kg in a week", "doctor-approved" without basis. Supplements are not medicines.
- **Weight-loss products and similar**: typically restricted to 18+ audiences.
- **Prescription drugs, online pharmacies and some health services** require platform certification or are prohibited; supplements with drug-like ingredients may be banned in some countries.
- **No fake doctors, fake news articles, fake celebrity endorsements or fake reviews.** These are common in nutra and are exactly what gets accounts restricted and brands sued.
- The landing page and the Telegram/WhatsApp content must also follow the rules; policies cover the destination.

### Compliant creative alternatives
| Prohibited | Compliant alternative |
|---|---|
| Before/after photo | Product in use, ingredient close-ups, routine demos |
| "Are you overweight?" | "A morning routine built around simple habits" |
| "Cures joint pain" | "Ingredients people use as part of their joint-care routine" (if claims are permitted for that ingredient locally) |
| Fake doctor | Real, disclosed creator explaining their own routine, no medical claims |
| "Lose 10 kg in 2 weeks" | Focus on habits, taste, convenience, product quality |

Ingredient and health claims are regulated differently in each country. If the brand has an approved claims list, use only those claims.

## 3. Funnels with Telegram and chat-based consultation

### Why a chat funnel
- In many markets, people prefer to ask questions before buying. A consultation in chat raises trust and approve/buyout rates compared with a cold form.
- Telegram bots and channels give you a direct line for reminders, delivery updates and repeat orders.

### Typical flows
1. **Ad → Telegram bot**: bot asks a few questions (goal, preferred contact time, city), gives general product information, takes the order (name, phone, address) or hands off to a human consultant. Track bot Starts in Joinvoo.
2. **Ad → channel → bot/consultant**: channel builds trust with product education and real customer content (with consent), posts link to the bot for orders.
3. **Ad → landing page → chat**: landing page with order form; after the order, the confirmation offers a Telegram link for delivery updates.

### Consultation best practice
- Consultants are product advisers, not doctors. They must not diagnose or promise medical outcomes. Advise users with medical conditions, pregnancy or medications to ask a healthcare professional.
- Answer fast: reply speed strongly affects approve rate. An AI closer like Replyvoo (a Zedapex tool) can answer common questions instantly and pass hot leads to humans.
- Confirm price, quantity, delivery time and payment method clearly. Surprises at the door kill buyout.
- Follow up on delivery day and after delivery (usage tips, satisfaction check, reorder offer). Castvoo-style broadcasts to bot users can handle this.

## 4. Angles

### Generally effective and compliant
- Natural ingredients and how the product is made (truthful).
- Routine and lifestyle: "my morning routine", "what I take before the gym" from real, disclosed creators.
- Convenience: easy to take, tastes good, fast delivery, pay on delivery.
- Trust: real company, real address, clear returns policy.
- Local relevance: local language, local creators, local delivery.

### Avoid
- Miracle results, scientific-sounding fake studies.
- Fear-based angles about diseases.
- Fake scarcity ("only 3 left today").
- Pretending to be a news outlet or government health body.

## 5. Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CPL (cost per lead) | spend ÷ leads | Top-of-funnel efficiency |
| Approve rate | approved ÷ leads | Lead quality and call centre performance |
| Cost per approved lead | spend ÷ approved | What you compare with the payout per approved lead |
| Buyout rate | paid orders ÷ approved | Expectation match, delivery speed |
| Cost per paid order | spend ÷ paid orders | True cost for straight-sale-like economics |
| Click→Start (bot) or click→join | Starts or joins ÷ clicks | Chat funnel friction |

### Targets
- **Break-even CPL (COD, paid per approve)** = payout per approved lead × approve rate.
- **Target CPL** = break-even CPL × (1 − margin).
- If the program adjusts payouts for buyout, use: payout per approved lead × approve rate × (adjustment factor based on buyout) — ask the manager how they calculate it.
- For straight sale: break-even cost per sale = payout per sale (or product margin if you own the product), minus refunds and chargebacks.

### Reading the numbers
- **Cheap leads, low approve**: wrong audience, bait creative, fake or mistyped phone numbers, bot submissions. Add a confirmation step in the bot, check suspect clicks, refine the angle.
- **Good approve, low buyout**: price or product surprise at delivery, slow delivery, too-aggressive upsell by the call centre. Align creative with the real offer.
- **Good approve and buyout, high cost**: creative or CPM issue; see campaign-fundamentals.md.

## 6. Geos
- Nutra is strong where COD is common and trust in online payment is lower. Check that the product is legal and registered (where required) in the target country.
- Local language and local creators matter a lot.
- Delivery coverage: limit targeting to areas the courier serves; out-of-area leads become rejects.

---

## 7. Common mistakes
1. Before/after images, body shaming and personal attribute callouts in ads.
2. Medical claims and fake doctors.
3. Trials with hidden recurring billing.
4. Judging on CPL alone, ignoring approve and buyout.
5. Slow responses to leads.
6. Targeting areas outside delivery coverage.
7. Creative promises that differ from the product delivered.
8. Fake reviews in the channel or on landing pages.

## 8. Launch plan for a COD offer with chat consultation

1. Confirm the product is legal in the geo and get the brand's approved claims list.
2. Bot: Start → short product info → 2–3 questions → order form (name, phone, city) → confirmation message with price and delivery time. Track Starts in Joinvoo; send `lead` on order, `qualified` on approve, `sale` on buyout, `rejected` on refusal/return.
3. 3 compliant angles (routine, ingredients, convenience) × 2–3 hooks; broad targeting within delivery coverage; age per policy.
4. Days 3–5: rank by cost per approved lead, not CPL. Kill at about 2–3× target cost per approved lead.
5. Week 2: ask the program for buyout data per sub ID; shift budget to ads with good buyout.

## 9. Joe's quick answers
- "Can I use a before/after photo if it's real?" → For weight loss, skin, hair and similar health results, before/after images are prohibited on Meta and similar on other platforms, even if real. Show the product, routine or ingredients instead.
- "My approve rate dropped." → Check for new ads with bait hooks, fake or mistyped numbers, bot submissions (suspect clicks), and call centre response time. Add a confirmation step in the bot.
- "Can the consultant say it cures X?" → No. Consultants give product information, not medical advice, and must not make disease claims.
- "Should I add a timer to the landing page?" → Only if the offer genuinely ends. Fake countdowns break consumer rules and platform policies.
